01 · The one-page summary

The short version

CDC doesn't have a technology problem so much as a paperwork problem. Every job gets typed in three times, and the paper copy becomes the real record. Fix that one thing and most of the other headaches get smaller.

The core problem

A job is entered in Seazona, written on the dentist's slip, then retyped into a Word lab slip. The paper then sits on the floor with the work, so:

  • Seazona is out of date most of the time.
  • Cases are hard to find without walking the floor.
  • Everything runs through one coordinator, Roo.

The two main paths

Howll AI has quoted $30,000 + GST for a stage one custom build.

Casetop, an off-the-shelf dental lab system, appears to solve most of the same problems for about US$349 a month, with no contract and a free trial.

Why this matters

Howll has correctly named the problems. The question is whether CDC needs to pay to build something new, or whether a product already built for dental labs can do the job for a fraction of the cost. Based on what's public, Casetop looks like it covers most of the list. The gaps (Xero invoicing, skill-based scheduling and Australian hosting) are real, and the questions have been put to Casetop.

Nothing here needs a quick decision. A trial costs nothing, runs alongside Seazona and will tell you more in a month than any proposal can.

Recommendation

Run a 30-day Casetop trial on real cases before signing anything with Howll.

04 · A note on the Howll quote

Fair, but ask before you sign

Howll is a real Perth business and the problems it identified are genuine. The original $7,500 step was sensible: map the process first, then price the build. The revised quote landed exactly at CDC's $30k cap by leaving out the hardest part (scheduling) and trading the discount for partner rights. If you do go ahead, ask for these first:

  1. Ownership of the system and code, in writing.
  2. A clear definition of the partner commitment.
  3. Milestones and a timeline up front.
  4. Exactly what the Seazona API connection will cover.
  5. The full terms, including the cut-off sentence.
  6. Referees with live systems you can talk to.
  7. A written extension of the quote, if you need more time.
05 · Why not lock in now

Stay flexible while AI tools move fast

AI tools are moving fast. Something that costs $30,000 to build today may be a standard feature in an off-the-shelf product next year. It's better to stay month to month, own your process map, and avoid long lock-ins. That keeps CDC free to switch as better options turn up.
